For seventy years the Turing test was a conversation. Could a machine type well enough that you could not tell it from the person on the other end. We passed that quietly, somewhere between two model releases, and almost nobody stopped to notice.

What is happening now is bigger. The test is falling in every sense at once.

## Sight

A year ago you could spot generated video in a second. The hands were wrong, the light did not behave, faces drifted between frames. Today most people cannot tell, and even the people building these tools get fooled. The tell is gone, and with it the old idea that seeing is believing.

## Sound

Voice went the same way, only faster. The pauses, the breaths, the small corrections people make in the middle of a sentence are all there now, in real time, on an ordinary phone call. The first time you realise after a call that you were talking to an agent, it does not feel like a breakthrough. It feels normal, and that is exactly what makes it one.

## Thought

Text was first, and it keeps moving. Models now work through problems that used to take a team a week, and they do it in the voice of whoever asked. The question was never whether a machine could sound human. It was whether it could think alongside one, and that line is behind us too.

## After the test

Turing asked whether machines can think, and turned it into a game because the real question was too hard to answer. The game is now over in every modality, and the real question is back. Not whether it can pass for us, but what it should do once nobody can tell the difference. I think the next decade belongs to whoever answers that with trust rather than tricks, agents that are clear about what they are, remember who they work for, and earn the right to act on our behalf.
