For three years we have used AI as a conversation. You open a chat, explain yourself, get an answer, and it forgets you. That era is ending. The next one is personal. An AI that knows who you are, what you are working on and what you promised last week, and keeps working while you sleep. Instinct.co is the first product I have used that feels like that, and it works because one team designed everything underneath it as a single, vertically integrated system.

Most agent companies today are not building minds. They are building plumbing. A vector database for memory, a sandbox for code, a workflow engine for crashes, a regular database for everything else. Four systems from four vendors, held together with glue code that has to be rewritten every time the models change. You cannot build something personal out of parts that don't know about each other.

## Latency stopped mattering

Databases were built for people, so we spent decades making them answer in milliseconds, and those milliseconds disappear next to the seconds a model spends thinking. What agents need instead is to run for days, survive failures, and keep every step they take. A context window is not memory, however long it gets. It is gone the moment the task ends.

Now multiply that by billions of agents, each writing thousands of steps a day that have to be kept for a lifetime and found again in milliseconds. That is not a database load, it is a planet's worth of memory, and nothing we built for SaaS was designed or priced for it.

Every computing era was won by whoever integrated what everyone else kept separate. The PC put the chip, the operating system and the software into one box you could put on a desk, and the iPhone put the phone, the camera, the music and the internet into something that fit in your pocket. The cloud did the same for servers, hiding a thousand machines behind a single button. Nobody remembers the parts of any of them, only that they just worked, and I think agents are next, with the companies stitching together four systems losing to the one that finally makes them one.

## What outlives the model

A model's weights are fixed once training ends, and retraining one for every person on Earth is not how this scales. Everything that makes an agent yours comes after that. The way you write, the people you trust, the thing you said you would never do again. None of it lives in the model. It lives in storage, and while we are spending hundreds of billions of dollars to make models smarter, what goes into the system an agent actually lives in, where its memory, the computer it works on and the work that has to survive for weeks are built as one thing, is a rounding error by comparison. That is why every agent still wakes up a brilliant stranger.

I think superintelligence will be personal before it is general. Not one giant model that knows everything about the world, but a mind that knows everything about you, built from years of state it never lets go of, with whichever model is best that year reading from it. The model becomes a part you swap every six months, and the memory is the part that grows with you. A lifetime of state for every person can only live on blob storage like S3, and databases like SlateDB, built directly on top of it, are the first pieces of a new kind of infrastructure made for minds rather than web apps. What stands between us and that isn't more intelligence. It's memory, a place to act, and time, for the 8 billion agents being built.
